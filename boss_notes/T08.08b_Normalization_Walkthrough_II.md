# T08.8b: Table Normalization Walkthroughs II (2020 & 2023)

> **Topic:** T08 — Normalization & Functional Dependencies | **Priority:** 🟠 | **Exam Frequency:** 4/7 finals (27 marks total across T08.8)
> **Sources:** Silberschatz 7e Ch. 7 (§7.1, §7.3, §7.8) · Lecture Deck 06 (Slides 30–40) · Raidah Notes (p. 22–23)

## Why This Topic Matters

Semester final exams often test your normalization skills with trickier real-world tables that include merged cells, calculated columns, or parallel comma-separated lists. For example, Question 8(b) in 2020 (8 marks) featured merged project rows, a calculated total charge column, and a part asking you to explain the table's anomalies. Similarly, Question 2(a) in 2023 (6 marks) gave a student table where courses, instructors, and grades were all packed into parallel comma-separated lists. Once you know how to clean up these quirks during the 1NF step, the rest of the 2NF and 3NF decomposition becomes completely straightforward.

---

## 📝 Key Definitions

> **First Normal Form (1NF):** A relation schema $R$ is in First Normal Form (1NF) if all of its attributes have atomic domains. Every cell must store a single scalar value with no comma-separated lists, merged multi-row cells, or repeating groups.

> **Second Normal Form (2NF):** A relation schema $R$ is in Second Normal Form (2NF) if it is in 1NF and has no partial functional dependencies. No non-prime attribute may depend on a proper subset of a composite candidate key.

> **Third Normal Form (3NF):** A relation schema $R$ is in Third Normal Form (3NF) if it is in 2NF and has no transitive dependencies where one non-prime attribute determines another non-prime attribute. Formally, for every $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$ in $F^+$, either $\beta \subseteq \alpha$, $\alpha$ is a superkey of $R$, or each attribute in $\beta - \alpha$ belongs to a candidate key.

## Handling Advanced Table Complexities

Exam tables often contain formatting quirks from paper forms or spreadsheets that you must clean up right at the start of your 1NF step.

### Dealing with Merged Cells (Rowspan)

In printed reports or spreadsheets, repeating values are often merged visually across multiple rows (using `rowspan` in HTML). However, in a relational database, every row must be a complete, self-contained record with no missing cells.

**How to fix:**
Copy the merged value down into every row that it spans. For example, if Project `13` (`Banking`) spans two employee rows, write `13` and `Banking` explicitly in both rows when creating your 1NF table.

### Detecting and Dropping Derived Attributes

A derived attribute is a column whose value can be calculated directly from other columns in the same row, such as:

$$
\text{Total Charge} = \text{Chg/Hr} \times \text{Hrs\_Billed}
$$

Storing calculated columns in a base table causes two problems:
1. It wastes disk space on numbers the database can easily compute when needed.
2. It creates update anomalies if someone updates `Chg/Hr` or `Hrs_Billed` and forgets to recalculate `Total Charge`.

**How to fix:**
Drop the derived column during your 1NF conversion and state clearly in your exam script why you dropped it. Queries can always calculate `Chg_Hr * Hrs_Billed` on the fly in a `SELECT` clause.

### Unpacking Synchronized Multi-Valued Lists

Sometimes a single row has comma-separated lists in several columns at once, where the items match up by position:
- Column 1 (`Courses`): `Math, Physics`
- Column 2 (`Instructors`): `Dr. Smith, Dr. Brown`
- Column 3 (`Grades`): `A, B+`

**How to fix:**
Unpack the lists together item by item (1st with 1st, 2nd with 2nd). Here, the first row gets `(Math, Dr. Smith, A)` and the second row gets `(Physics, Dr. Brown, B+)`. Never take a cross product across the lists!

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)

### 🎯 GQ1 · 2020 Q.8(b) · Project Charges Table Normalization and Anomalies

> **Appeared:** 2020 Q.8(b) — (08 Marks)

**Question:**

Consider the given structure below:

<table border="1" cellpadding="4" style="border-collapse: collapse; text-align: center; margin-top: 6px; margin-bottom: 6px;"><thead><tr><th style="padding: 3px 6px;">Proj. Num.</th><th style="padding: 3px 6px;">Proj. Name</th><th style="padding: 3px 6px;">Emp. No.</th><th style="padding: 3px 6px;">Emp. Name</th><th style="padding: 3px 6px;">Job Class</th><th style="padding: 3px 6px;">Chg/Hr</th><th style="padding: 3px 6px;">Hrs. Biller.</th><th style="padding: 3px 6px;">Total Charge</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td rowspan="2">13</td><td rowspan="2">Banking</td><td>103</td><td>John</td><td style="text-align: left;">Analyst</td><td>500</td><td>8</td><td>4000</td></tr><tr><td>105</td><td>Steve</td><td style="text-align: left;">Programmer</td><td>300</td><td>8</td><td>2400</td></tr><tr><td rowspan="2">26</td><td rowspan="2">Telecom</td><td>115</td><td>Jim</td><td style="text-align: left;">Business Analyst</td><td>100</td><td>10</td><td>1000</td></tr><tr><td>717</td><td>Joe</td><td style="text-align: left;">Engineer</td><td>300</td><td>7</td><td>2100</td></tr><tr><td rowspan="2">39</td><td rowspan="2">Finance</td><td>113</td><td>David</td><td style="text-align: left;">Team Leader</td><td>1000</td><td>4</td><td>4000</td></tr><tr><td>120</td><td>Jian</td><td style="text-align: left;">DBA</td><td>800</td><td>3</td><td>2400</td></tr></tbody></table>  
(i) Identify the anomalies in the given structure.  
(ii) Normalize it up to third Normal Form (3NF).

*(Note: In the original examination paper, "Hrs. Billed" is printed as "Hrs. Biller.", and the `Proj. Num.` and `Proj. Name` cells span both employee rows for each project)*

*(Sorting Note: Part i asks to identify anomalies, cross-ref T08.1)*

**Full Answer:**

#### (i) Anomalies in the Given Structure

Because this table mixes project details, employee details, job billing rates, and hours worked into one big unnormalized structure, this unnormalized table suffers from three anomalies (along with unnecessary data redundancy):

1. **Data Redundancy:**
   - Project names (like `Banking` for Project 13) are repeated for every employee working on that project.
   - The hourly rate (`Chg/Hr`) is repeated for every employee who has the same `Job Class`.
   - `Total Charge` is a calculated column ($\text{Total Charge} = \text{Chg/Hr} \times \text{Hrs. Biller.}$) that wastes storage space.

2. **Update Anomaly:**
   - If the billing rate for a `Job Class` changes (for example, if `Programmer` increases from 300 to 350), we have to update every row where a programmer appears. If even one row is missed, the database ends up with conflicting rates for the same job.
   - Similarly, renaming a project requires updating multiple rows.

3. **Insertion Anomaly:**
   - We cannot add a newly approved project to the database until at least one employee is assigned to it, because `Emp. No.` is part of the composite primary key and cannot be `NULL`.
   - Likewise, we cannot record a new `Job Class` and its `Chg/Hr` rate until an employee with that job class is assigned to a project.

4. **Deletion Anomaly:**
   - If Project 39 (`Finance`) is cancelled and we delete its rows, we also accidentally wipe out all employee details (name, job class, and rate) for David (`113`) and Jian (`120`) if they are not currently working on any other project.

---

#### (ii) Normalization up to Third Normal Form (3NF)

*(Note: We write the column printed as "Hrs. Biller." as `Hrs_Billed`.)*

**Functional Dependencies in the Structure:**
- $\text{Proj\_Num} \rightarrow \text{Proj\_Name}$
- $\text{Emp\_No} \rightarrow \text{Emp\_Name}, \text{Job\_Class}$
- $\text{Job\_Class} \rightarrow \text{Chg\_Hr}$
- $(\text{Proj\_Num}, \text{Emp\_No}) \rightarrow \text{Hrs\_Billed}$
- $\text{Total Charge}$ is dropped because it is a derived attribute ($\text{Chg\_Hr} \times \text{Hrs\_Billed}$).

---

#### Step 1: 1NF Conversion for Project Charges

First, we fill down the merged `Proj_Num` and `Proj_Name` cells so that every row is complete on its own, and we drop the calculated `Total Charge` column:

| Proj_Num | Proj_Name | Emp_No | Emp_Name | Job_Class | Chg_Hr | Hrs_Billed |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| 13 | Banking | 103 | John | Analyst | 500 | 8 |
| 13 | Banking | 105 | Steve | Programmer | 300 | 8 |
| 26 | Telecom | 115 | Jim | Business Analyst | 100 | 10 |
| 26 | Telecom | 717 | Joe | Engineer | 300 | 7 |
| 39 | Finance | 113 | David | Team Leader | 1000 | 4 |
| 39 | Finance | 120 | Jian | DBA | 800 | 3 |

**Primary Key for 1NF:** `(Proj_Num, Emp_No)`

---

#### Step 2: 2NF Decomposition for Project Charges

The 1NF relation has a composite primary key `(Proj_Num, Emp_No)`.
We identify partial functional dependencies:
- $\text{Proj\_Num} \rightarrow \text{Proj\_Name}$ (Depends only on subset `Proj_Num`.)
- $\text{Emp\_No} \rightarrow \text{Emp\_Name}, \text{Job\_Class}, \text{Chg\_Hr}$ (Depends only on subset `Emp_No`.)

We decompose the 1NF table into three relations to eliminate partial dependencies:

1. **PROJECT**

| Proj_Num | Proj_Name |
| :---: | :--- |
| 13 | Banking |
| 26 | Telecom |
| 39 | Finance |

Primary Key: `Proj_Num`.

2. **EMPLOYEE_2NF**

| Emp_No | Emp_Name | Job_Class | Chg_Hr |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| 103 | John | Analyst | 500 |
| 105 | Steve | Programmer | 300 |
| 115 | Jim | Business Analyst | 100 |
| 717 | Joe | Engineer | 300 |
| 113 | David | Team Leader | 1000 |
| 120 | Jian | DBA | 800 |

Primary Key: `Emp_No`.

3. **ASSIGNMENT**

| Proj_Num | Emp_No | Hrs_Billed |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 13 | 103 | 8 |
| 13 | 105 | 8 |
| 26 | 115 | 10 |
| 26 | 717 | 7 |
| 39 | 113 | 4 |
| 39 | 120 | 3 |

Primary Key: `(Proj_Num, Emp_No)`. Foreign Keys: `Proj_Num` references `PROJECT(Proj_Num)`, `Emp_No` references `EMPLOYEE_2NF(Emp_No)`.

---

#### Step 3: 3NF Decomposition for Project Charges

In `EMPLOYEE_2NF`, a transitive functional dependency exists:

$$
\text{Emp\_No} \rightarrow \text{Job\_Class} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Job\_Class} \rightarrow \text{Chg\_Hr}
$$

`Job_Class` is a non-prime attribute determining another non-prime attribute `Chg_Hr`.
To eliminate this transitive dependency, we decompose `EMPLOYEE_2NF` into two relations:
1. `JOB(Job_Class, Chg_Hr)` with Primary Key: `Job_Class`.
2. `EMPLOYEE(Emp_No, Emp_Name, Job_Class)` with Primary Key: `Emp_No`. Foreign Key: `Job_Class` references `JOB(Job_Class)`.

---

#### Final 3NF Relations and Populated Data

1. **PROJECT**

| Proj_Num | Proj_Name |
| :---: | :--- |
| 13 | Banking |
| 26 | Telecom |
| 39 | Finance |

Primary Key: `Proj_Num`.

2. **JOB**

| Job_Class | Chg_Hr |
| :--- | :---: |
| Analyst | 500 |
| Programmer | 300 |
| Business Analyst | 100 |
| Engineer | 300 |
| Team Leader | 1000 |
| DBA | 800 |

Primary Key: `Job_Class`.

3. **EMPLOYEE**

| Emp_No | Emp_Name | Job_Class |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| 103 | John | Analyst |
| 105 | Steve | Programmer |
| 115 | Jim | Business Analyst |
| 717 | Joe | Engineer |
| 113 | David | Team Leader |
| 120 | Jian | DBA |

Primary Key: `Emp_No`.  
Foreign Key: `Job_Class` references `JOB(Job_Class)`.

4. **ASSIGNMENT**

| Proj_Num | Emp_No | Hrs_Billed |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 13 | 103 | 8 |
| 13 | 105 | 8 |
| 26 | 115 | 10 |
| 26 | 717 | 7 |
| 39 | 113 | 4 |
| 39 | 120 | 3 |

Primary Key: `(Proj_Num, Emp_No)`.  
Foreign Keys: `Proj_Num` references `PROJECT(Proj_Num)`, `Emp_No` references `EMPLOYEE(Emp_No)`.

### 🎯 GQ2 · 2023 Q.2(a) · University Student Course Grades Normalization

> **Appeared:** 2023 Q.2(a) — (06 Marks)

**Question:**

Consider a university tracking students, their courses and exam scores. A sample table is given below. Step by step convert this to 3NF.

<table border="1" style="border-collapse: collapse; text-align: center;"><thead><tr><th style="padding: 4px 10px;">S_ID</th><th style="padding: 4px 10px;">S-Name</th><th style="padding: 4px 10px;">Courses</th><th style="padding: 4px 10px;">Instructors</th><th style="padding: 4px 10px;">Grades</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">1</td><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">Alice</td><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">Math, Physics</td><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">Dr. Smith, Dr. Brown</td><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">A, B+</td></tr><tr><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">2</td><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">Bob</td><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">Chemistry</td><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">Dr. White</td><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">A-</td></tr><tr><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">3</td><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">Charlie</td><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">Math, Biology, Chemistry</td><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">Dr. Smith, Dr. Gray, Dr. White</td><td style="padding: 4px 10px;">B+, A-, A</td></tr></tbody></table>

**Full Answer:**

#### Step 1: 1NF Conversion for University Student Records

The given table violates 1NF because `Courses`, `Instructors`, and `Grades` hold comma-separated lists of values.
To convert it to 1NF, we unpack the three lists side by side in matching order:
- Alice (`1`): `(Math, Dr. Smith, A)` and `(Physics, Dr. Brown, B+)`.
- Bob (`2`): `(Chemistry, Dr. White, A-)`.
- Charlie (`3`): `(Math, Dr. Smith, B+)`, `(Biology, Dr. Gray, A-)`, and `(Chemistry, Dr. White, A)`.

This gives us the following 1NF table:

| S_ID | S-Name | Course | Instructor | Grade |
| :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: |
| 1 | Alice | Math | Dr. Smith | A |
| 1 | Alice | Physics | Dr. Brown | B+ |
| 2 | Bob | Chemistry | Dr. White | A- |
| 3 | Charlie | Math | Dr. Smith | B+ |
| 3 | Charlie | Biology | Dr. Gray | A- |
| 3 | Charlie | Chemistry | Dr. White | A |

**Primary Key for 1NF:** `(S_ID, Course)`

**Functional Dependencies Identified:**
1. $\text{S\_ID} \rightarrow \text{S-Name}$ (Partial dependency on `S_ID` alone.)
2. $\text{Course} \rightarrow \text{Instructor}$ (Partial dependency on `Course` alone, since each course is taught by one instructor.)
3. $(\text{S\_ID}, \text{Course}) \rightarrow \text{Grade}$ (Full dependency on the composite key.)

---

#### Step 2: 2NF Decomposition for University Student Records

Because our 1NF table has two partial dependencies on parts of `(S_ID, Course)` (`S-Name` depends only on `S_ID`, and `Instructor` depends only on `Course`), we split the table into three 2NF relations:

1. **STUDENT**

| S_ID | S-Name |
| :---: | :--- |
| 1 | Alice |
| 2 | Bob |
| 3 | Charlie |

Primary Key: `S_ID`.

2. **COURSE**

| Course | Instructor |
| :--- | :--- |
| Math | Dr. Smith |
| Physics | Dr. Brown |
| Chemistry | Dr. White |
| Biology | Dr. Gray |

Primary Key: `Course`.

3. **ENROLLMENT**

| S_ID | Course | Grade |
| :---: | :--- | :---: |
| 1 | Math | A |
| 1 | Physics | B+ |
| 2 | Chemistry | A- |
| 3 | Math | B+ |
| 3 | Biology | A- |
| 3 | Chemistry | A |

Primary Key: `(S_ID, Course)`.  
Foreign Keys: `S_ID` references `STUDENT(S_ID)`, `Course` references `COURSE(Course)`.

Every non-prime attribute in these three tables now depends on the entire candidate key of its table, so the schema is in 2NF.

---

#### Step 3: Evaluation and Verification of Third Normal Form (3NF)

Finally, let's check whether any of our 2NF tables has a transitive dependency (where one non-key column determines another non-key column):
- In `STUDENT(S_ID, S-Name)`, there is only one non-key column (`S-Name`), which depends directly on `S_ID`.
- In `COURSE(Course, Instructor)`, there is only one non-key column (`Instructor`), which depends directly on `Course`.
- In `ENROLLMENT(S_ID, Course, Grade)`, there is only one non-key column (`Grade`), which depends directly on `(S_ID, Course)`.

Because none of these tables has a non-key attribute determining another non-key attribute, all three tables are already in **3NF**.

#### Final 3NF Schema Summary

$$
\text{STUDENT}(\underline{\text{S\_ID}}, \text{S-Name})
$$

$$
\text{COURSE}(\underline{\text{Course}}, \text{Instructor})
$$

$$
\text{ENROLLMENT}(\underline{\text{S\_ID}, \text{Course}}, \text{Grade})
$$

- **Primary Keys:** `S_ID` in `STUDENT`, `Course` in `COURSE`, and `(S_ID, Course)` in `ENROLLMENT`.
- **Foreign Keys:** In `ENROLLMENT`, `S_ID` references `STUDENT(S_ID)` and `Course` references `COURSE(Course)`.
- **Lossless Join:** Joining `STUDENT`, `COURSE`, and `ENROLLMENT` on their primary keys restores the exact 1NF table without generating any spurious tuples.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

1. **Fill Down Merged Cells Completely:** In 2020 Q.8(b), project number 13 and project name Banking apply to both employee rows. Ensure every row in your 1NF table has explicit values in every column before writing the primary key.
2. **Always Drop Calculated Columns:** Never create a separate table for `Total Charge`. Explicitly state in your answer: "Total Charge is dropped because it is a calculated attribute ($\text{Chg/Hr} \times \text{Hrs\_Billed}$)."
3. **Trace Positional List Pairs Carefully:** In 2023 Q.2(a), match courses to instructors to grades by position. Do not combine Charlie's Math course with Dr. Gray or Grade A.
4. **State Clearly When 2NF Already Meets 3NF:** If no transitive dependencies exist in your 2NF tables, write a short paragraph explaining that the 2NF schema already satisfies 3NF. Do not invent unnecessary extra tables.
5. **Always Underline Primary Keys:** Underlining primary keys in schema notation ($\underline{\text{PK}}$) makes your answer easy to grade and guarantees clarity.
