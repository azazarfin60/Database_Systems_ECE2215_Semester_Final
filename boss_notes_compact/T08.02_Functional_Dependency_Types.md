# T08.2: Functional Dependencies and Attribute Classification

> **Topic:** T08 · Normalization & Functional Dependencies | **Priority:** 🟢 Foundational | **Exam Frequency:** 0/7 finals · CT-03 Q01(b–c) (4 marks)
> **Sources:** Silberschatz 7e Ch. 7 (§7.2, §7.2.2) · Lecture Deck 06 (Slides 14–17) · Raidah Class Notes (p. 20)

## Understanding Functional Dependencies

**Functional Dependency:** A formal constraint between two attribute sets in a relation schema $R$. Let $\alpha \subseteq R$ and $\beta \subseteq R$. The functional dependency $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$ holds on $R$ if, in every legal relation instance $r(R)$, any two tuples $t_1$ and $t_2$ agreeing on $\alpha$ must also agree on $\beta$:

$$
t_1[\alpha] = t_2[\alpha] \implies t_1[\beta] = t_2[\beta]
$$

We say $\alpha$ **functionally determines** $\beta$. Here, $\alpha$ is the **determinant** and $\beta$ is the **dependent**.

Consider a student relation with tuples:

| Student_ID | Student_Name | Department |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| S01 | Ferdous | CSE |
| S02 | Zaman | ETE |
| S03 | Nabila | ECE |

Here, $\text{Student\_ID} \rightarrow \text{Student\_Name}$ holds because each distinct ID maps to exactly one name. If a new tuple arrives with ID `S01` and name `Kamal`, the rule is broken.

## Classification of Functional Dependencies

Functional dependencies fall into distinct categories based on set containment, candidate key coverage, and chaining.

### 1. By Set Containment: Trivial vs. Non-Trivial

**Trivial Functional Dependency:** An FD $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$ where the right-hand side is a subset of the left-hand side ($\beta \subseteq \alpha$). Trivial dependencies are satisfied by every legal relation instance and provide no new constraint.
- Examples: $\{\text{StudentID}, \text{Name}\} \rightarrow \text{StudentID}$, $A \rightarrow A$, $ABC \rightarrow AB$.

**Non-Trivial Functional Dependency:** An FD $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$ where determinant and dependent share no attributes ($\alpha \cap \beta = \emptyset$).
- Examples: $\text{StudentID} \rightarrow \text{Name}$, $\text{CourseID} \rightarrow \text{Credits}$.

**Semi-Trivial Functional Dependency:** An FD $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$ where $\beta$ contains some attributes from $\alpha$ plus new attributes ($\beta \not\subseteq \alpha$ and $\alpha \cap \beta \neq \emptyset$).
- Example: $\{A, B\} \rightarrow \{B, C\}$. By decomposition, this yields trivial $\{A, B\} \rightarrow B$ and non-trivial $\{A, B\} \rightarrow C$.

### 2. By Key Coverage: Full vs. Partial Dependency

**Full Functional Dependency:** An attribute set $Y$ is fully functionally dependent on $X$ if $X \rightarrow Y$ holds and no proper subset of $X$ determines $Y$:

$$
\forall A \in X, \quad (X - \{A\}) \not\rightarrow Y
$$

- Example: In an enrollment table, $\{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}\} \rightarrow \text{Grade}$ is a full functional dependency. Neither $\text{StudentID}$ nor $\text{CourseID}$ alone determines a student's grade in a course.

**Partial Dependency:** A functional dependency where a non-prime attribute $Y$ depends on a proper subset $A \subset K$ of a composite candidate key $K$:

$$
\exists A \subset K \quad \text{such that} \quad A \rightarrow Y \quad (Y \notin K)
$$

- Example: If the composite key is $\{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}\}$, the dependency $\text{StudentID} \rightarrow \text{StudentName}$ is partial because $\text{StudentName}$ depends only on $\text{StudentID}$. Partial dependencies violate Second Normal Form (2NF).

### 3. By Chaining: Transitive Dependency

**Transitive Dependency:** A condition where a candidate key $K$ determines a non-prime attribute $Z$ indirectly through another non-prime attribute $Y$:

$$
K \longrightarrow Y \longrightarrow Z \quad (Y \not\rightarrow K, \; Z \notin Y)
$$

- Example: In $\text{STUDENT}(\text{Student\_ID}, \text{Student\_Name}, \text{Dept\_ID}, \text{Dept\_Name})$ with candidate key $\{\text{Student\_ID}\}$:
  - $\text{Student\_ID} \rightarrow \text{Dept\_ID}$
  - $\text{Dept\_ID} \rightarrow \text{Dept\_Name}$
  Here, $\text{Dept\_Name}$ depends transitively on $\text{Student\_ID}$ through non-prime attribute $\text{Dept\_ID}$. Transitive dependencies violate Third Normal Form (3NF).

## Prime vs. Non-Prime Attributes

Every attribute in a schema belongs to one of two mutually exclusive sets:

**Prime Attribute:** Any attribute that is a member of at least one candidate key of the relation.

**Non-Prime Attribute:** Any attribute that does not belong to any candidate key of the relation.

### Classification Steps

1. Find all candidate keys of schema $R$.
2. Take the union of all candidate keys: $\text{Prime}(R) = \bigcup CK_i$.
3. All remaining attributes are non-prime: $\text{NonPrime}(R) = R - \text{Prime}(R)$.

> [!WARNING]
> **The Determinant Trap:** Being on the left-hand side of an arrow does not make an attribute prime. In $\text{InstructorID} \rightarrow \text{InstructorName}$, $\text{InstructorID}$ is a determinant. If $\text{InstructorID}$ does not belong to any candidate key, it is still a non-prime attribute.

### Master Summary of Dependency Types

| Dependency Type | Mathematical Condition | Normal Form Violated | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Trivial** | $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$ where $\beta \subseteq \alpha$ | None (valid everywhere) | Retain (no action needed) |
| **Partial** | $A \rightarrow Y$ where $A \subset CK$ and $Y \in \text{NonPrime}$ | Violated in 1NF (prevents 2NF) | Move $A$ and $Y$ to a new table |
| **Transitive** | $Y \rightarrow Z$ where $Y, Z \in \text{NonPrime}$ and $CK \rightarrow Y$ | Violated in 2NF (prevents 3NF) | Move $Y$ and $Z$ to a new table |
| **Full** | $X \rightarrow Y$ and $\forall A \subset X, (X - \{A\}) \not\rightarrow Y$ | Satisfies 2NF requirements | Keep together in table |

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)

### 🎯 GQ1 · CT-03 Q.01(b–c) · Prime Attributes and Dependency Classification · 04 Marks

> *CO3 · Asked in: CT-03*

**Question.** Consider $\text{ENROLLMENT}(\text{StudentID}, \text{StudentName}, \text{CourseID}, \text{CourseName}, \text{InstructorID}, \text{InstructorName}, \text{Grade})$ with functional dependencies: $\text{StudentID} \rightarrow \text{StudentName}$; $\text{CourseID} \rightarrow \text{CourseName}, \text{InstructorID}$; $\text{InstructorID} \rightarrow \text{InstructorName}$; $(\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}) \rightarrow \text{Grade}$.  
**(b)** Identify the prime and non-prime attributes. `[02 Marks]`  
**(c)** Identify the partial and transitive dependencies. `[02 Marks]`

**Answer.**

First, we determine the Candidate Key.

Attributes $\text{StudentID}$ and $\text{CourseID}$ never appear on the right-hand side of any functional dependency. Therefore, every candidate key must contain both attributes.

Computing the closure:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}\}^+ &= \{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}, \text{StudentName}, \text{CourseName}, \text{InstructorID}, \text{InstructorName}, \text{Grade}\}
\end{aligned}
$$

Because no proper subset is a superkey, the unique Candidate Key is:
$$
\text{Candidate Key} = \{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}\}
$$

#### (b) Prime and Non-Prime Attributes

- **Prime Attributes:** Attributes that belong to at least one Candidate Key:
$$
\text{Prime Attributes} = \{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}\}
$$
- **Non-Prime Attributes:** Attributes that do not belong to any Candidate Key:
$$
\text{Non-Prime Attributes} = \{\text{StudentName}, \text{CourseName}, \text{InstructorID}, \text{InstructorName}, \text{Grade}\}
$$

#### (c) Partial and Transitive Dependencies

- **Partial Dependencies:** Occur when a non-prime attribute depends on a proper subset of a composite candidate key:
  1. $\text{StudentID} \rightarrow \text{StudentName}$ (the non-prime attribute $\text{StudentName}$ depends only on proper subset $\text{StudentID}$).
  2. $\text{CourseID} \rightarrow \text{CourseName}$ (the non-prime attribute $\text{CourseName}$ depends only on proper subset $\text{CourseID}$).
  3. $\text{CourseID} \rightarrow \text{InstructorID}$ (the non-prime attribute $\text{InstructorID}$ depends only on proper subset $\text{CourseID}$).

- **Transitive Dependencies:** Occur when a non-prime attribute depends on another non-prime attribute:
$$
\text{InstructorID} \rightarrow \text{InstructorName}
$$
  Here, $\text{InstructorID}$ is a non-prime attribute that determines the non-prime attribute $\text{InstructorName}$. From the primary key, we have $(\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}) \rightarrow \text{InstructorID} \rightarrow \text{InstructorName}$.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

- **Find all candidate keys first:** You cannot classify prime and non-prime attributes until you have found every candidate key. If you miss a candidate key, you will misclassify its attributes as non-prime.
- **Single-attribute keys have no partial dependencies:** If every candidate key in a table has only one attribute, partial dependencies are impossible. The table is automatically in 2NF.
- **Transitive dependency check:** Ensure the intermediate attribute is non-prime. If the determinant is a superkey, the dependency satisfies 3NF and BCNF.
