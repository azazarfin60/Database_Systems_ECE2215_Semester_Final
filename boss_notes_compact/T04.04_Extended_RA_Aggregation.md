## The Aggregate Operation ($\gamma$)

**Aggregate operation ($\gamma$):** An extended relational algebra operator that partitions tuples into groups and evaluates aggregate summary functions over each group. Written as:

$$
{}_{G_1, G_2, \dots, G_n}\gamma_{F_1(A_1) \text{ as } B_1, \dots, F_m(A_m) \text{ as } B_m}(E)
$$

**Aggregate functions:** Mathematical summary functions (`count`, `sum`, `avg`, `min`, `max`) that evaluate over a multiset of values (preserving duplicates) to produce a single aggregated value.

Here:
- $E$ is any relation or relational algebra expression.
- $G_1, \dots, G_n$ is an optional list of **grouping attributes** in the left subscript (equivalent to SQL `GROUP BY`).
- $F_i$ represents an aggregate function (`count`, `sum`, `avg`, `min`, `max`).
- $A_i$ is the attribute from $E$ being aggregated.
- $B_i$ is an optional alias name for the resulting summary attribute.

### 1. Global Aggregation (Empty Left Subscript)

When the left subscript is omitted, the entire relation is treated as a single group, producing one summary row:

- Average salary across all instructors:

$$
\gamma_{\text{avg}(\text{salary})}(\text{instructor})
$$

- Total count of courses in 2018:

$$
\gamma_{\text{count}(\text{course\_id})}(\sigma_{\text{year} = 2018}(\text{section}))
$$

### 2. Grouped Aggregation (With Grouping Attributes)

When grouping attributes $G_1, \dots, G_n$ are present, tuples are partitioned by identical values on $G_1, \dots, G_n$. The output schema is $(G_1, \dots, G_n, B_1, \dots, B_m)$.

- Example: Average salary in each department:

$$
{}_{\text{dept\_name}}\gamma_{\text{avg}(\text{salary}) \text{ as } \text{avg\_salary}}(\text{instructor})
$$

### 3. Emulating SQL's `HAVING` Clause

Relational algebra emulates `HAVING` by wrapping a selection ($\sigma$) outside the aggregate operator $\gamma$:

$$
\sigma_{\text{avg\_salary} > 75000}\left({}_{\text{dept\_name}}\gamma_{\text{avg}(\text{salary}) \text{ as } \text{avg\_salary}}(\text{instructor})\right)
$$

### 4. Three-Step Sequence for "Entity with Maximum Count"

To find an entity achieving a maximum count without losing non-aggregated attributes:

1. **Step 1 (Count per Group):** Group by the entity and count rows into relation $C$:

$$
C \leftarrow {}_{\text{company\_name}}\gamma_{\text{count}(\text{person\_name}) \text{ as } \text{emp\_count}}(\text{works})
$$

2. **Step 2 (Scalar Maximum):** Extract the highest count across all groups into $M$:

$$
M \leftarrow \gamma_{\text{max}(\text{emp\_count}) \text{ as } \text{max\_count}}(C)
$$

3. **Step 3 (Match and Project):** Cross $C \times M$, filter where counts match, and project the entity:

$$
\Pi_{\text{company\_name}}(\sigma_{\text{emp\_count} = \text{max\_count}}(C \times M))
$$

## Past Exam Aggregation Applications

Aggregation appears inside multi-part questions across topics T04.2a, T04.2b, and T04.2c. Below are the complete standalone solutions for each past exam application of the aggregate operator ($\gamma$):

### Drill 1 · 2017 Q.2(a)(iv) · Company with the Most Employees · [02 Marks]

**Question.** From relation $\text{works}(\text{person\_name}, \text{company\_name}, \text{salary})$, find the company with most employees.

**Answer.**
Count employees per company, determine the maximum count, and project the matching company:

$$
C \leftarrow {}_{\text{company\_name}}\gamma_{\text{count}(\text{person\_name}) \text{ as } \text{num\_emp}}(\text{works})
$$

$$
M \leftarrow \gamma_{\text{max}(\text{num\_emp}) \text{ as } \text{max\_emp}}(C)
$$

$$
\Pi_{\text{company\_name}}(\sigma_{\text{num\_emp} = \text{max\_emp}}(C \times M))
$$

### Drill 2 · 2018 Q.3(a)(i–ii) · Insurance Accidents and Car Owners · [04 Marks]

**Question.** Given $\text{accident}(\text{report\_number}, \text{date}, \text{location})$, $\text{owns}(\text{driver\_id}, \text{license})$, and $\text{participated}(\text{driver\_id}, \text{car}, \text{report\_number}, \text{damage\_amount})$:  
(i) Find the total number of people who owned cars that were involved in accidents in 1989.  
(ii) Find the number of accidents in which the cars belonging to "John Smith" were involved.

**Answer.**

**(i) Total people owning cars involved in 1989 accidents:**

$$
T_1 \leftarrow \sigma_{\text{year}(\text{date}) = 1989}(\text{accident}) \bowtie \text{participated} \bowtie_{\text{participated.car} = \text{owns.license}} \text{owns}
$$

$$
\gamma_{\text{count-distinct}(\text{owns.driver\_id})}(T_1)
$$

**(ii) Number of accidents involving cars belonging to "John Smith":**

$$
T_{\text{john\_cars}} \leftarrow \sigma_{\text{name} = \text{"John Smith"}}(\text{person}) \bowtie \text{owns}
$$

$$
T_{\text{accidents}} \leftarrow T_{\text{john\_cars}} \bowtie_{\text{owns.license} = \text{participated.car}} \text{participated}
$$

$$
\gamma_{\text{count-distinct}(\text{report\_number})}(T_{\text{accidents}})
$$

### Drill 3 · 2019 Q.2(b)(ii) · Maximum Suspected Patients on a Date · [02 Marks]

**Question.** From $\text{History}(\text{Patient\_ID}, \text{Symptom}, \text{Date})$, consider that if any patient has high-fever symptom he/she will be suspected as corona patient. Find out such maximum number of patients in a date.

**Answer.**
Count high-fever patients grouped by Date, then find the overall maximum:

$$
T_{\text{daily}} \leftarrow {}_{\text{Date}}\gamma_{\text{count}(\text{Patient\_ID}) \text{ as } \text{patient\_count}}(\sigma_{\text{Symptom} = \text{"high-fever"}}(\text{History}))
$$

$$
\gamma_{\text{max}(\text{patient\_count})}(T_{\text{daily}})
$$

### Drill 4 · 2024 Q.2(b) · Employees Working on Multiple Projects · [04 Marks]

**Question.** From $\text{EMPLOYEE}(\text{EID}, \text{Name}, \text{Department}, \text{Salary})$ and $\text{WORKS\_ON}(\text{EID}, \text{PID}, \text{Hours})$, find employees working on multiple projects.

**Answer.**
Group `WORKS_ON` by `EID`, filter for project counts strictly greater than 1, and join with `EMPLOYEE`:

$$
T_{\text{counts}} \leftarrow {}_{\text{EID}}\gamma_{\text{count}(\text{PID}) \ \text{as} \ \text{proj\_count}}(\text{WORKS\_ON})
$$

$$
T_{\text{multi}} \leftarrow \sigma_{\text{proj\_count} > 1}(T_{\text{counts}})
$$

$$
\Pi_{\text{EID}, \text{Name}}(\text{EMPLOYEE} \bowtie T_{\text{multi}})
$$

### Drill 5 · CT-01 Q.04(viii) · Number of Students in Each Department · [01 Mark]

**Question.** From relation $\text{STUDENT}(\text{Student\_ID}, \text{Name}, \text{Dept\_Name}, \text{CGPA})$, determine the number of students in each department.

**Answer.**
Group `STUDENT` by department name and count student identifiers:

$$
{}_{\text{Dept\_Name}}\gamma_{\text{count}(\text{Student\_ID})}(\text{STUDENT})
$$

## 🔮 Likely Questions

### 🎯 LQ1 · Department with the Highest Average Salary · [04 Marks]

> *Theory & Aggregation Formulation Drill*

**Question.** Given relation $\text{instructor}(\text{ID}, \text{name}, \text{dept\_name}, \text{salary})$, write a relational algebra expression to find the department name that has the highest average salary.

**Answer.**

1. **Calculate average salary for each department:**

$$
\text{DeptAvg} \leftarrow {}_{\text{dept\_name}}\gamma_{\text{avg}(\text{salary}) \text{ as } \text{avg\_sal}}(\text{instructor})
$$

2. **Determine the maximum average salary across all departments:**

$$
\text{MaxSal} \leftarrow \gamma_{\text{max}(\text{avg\_sal}) \text{ as } \text{peak}}(\text{DeptAvg})
$$

3. **Match and project the winning department name:**

$$
\Pi_{\text{dept\_name}}(\sigma_{\text{avg\_sal} = \text{peak}}(\text{DeptAvg} \times \text{MaxSal}))
$$

### 🎯 LQ2 · Finding Maximum Without the Aggregate Operator ($\gamma$) · [04 Marks]

> *Classical Relational Algebra Expressive Power Drill*

**Question.** Can you find the maximum value of an attribute without using extended relational algebra ($\gamma$)? Explain how this is done using Cartesian product and set difference on relation $r(A)$.

**Answer.**

Yes. Classical relational algebra determines the maximum value of attribute $A$ by finding all values that are smaller than at least one other value in the relation, then subtracting them from the full set:

1. **Find values strictly smaller than another value in $r$:** Rename a copy of $r$ to $d$, cross $r \times \rho_d(r)$, and select tuples where $r.A < d.A$:

$$
\text{NotMax} \leftarrow \Pi_{r.A}(\sigma_{r.A < d.A}(r \times \rho_d(r)))
$$

2. **Subtract non-maximum values from all attribute values:**

$$
\text{MaxVal} \leftarrow \Pi_A(r) - \text{NotMax}
$$

*Significance:* While this set difference method finds extreme values of existing attributes, it cannot compute arithmetic sums, counts, or averages. The aggregate operator $\gamma$ is required for all mathematical aggregations.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

- **Left vs. Right Subscript Placement:** Always write grouping attributes in the **left subscript** and aggregate functions in the **right subscript** (${}_{\text{grouping}}\gamma_{\text{functions}}(E)$). Swapping their positions is a common error.
- **Empty Left Subscript for Scalar Results:** When computing a single overall total or maximum across the entire relation, leave the left subscript completely empty ($\gamma_{\text{count}(A)}(E)$).
- **Always Provide Output Column Aliases:** Use `as` inside the right subscript (${}_G\gamma_{\text{count}(A) \text{ as } C}(E)$) so outer $\sigma$ or $\Pi$ operators can reference the calculated column.
- **Project Before Counting Distinct Entities:** Because $\gamma$ preserves duplicates, apply $\Pi_{\text{driver\_id}}(E)$ first when counting unique entities before calling $\gamma_{\text{count}(\text{driver\_id})}$.
