# T08.9: Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF) and 3NF vs. BCNF

> **Topic:** T08 · Normalization & Functional Dependencies | **Priority:** 🟢 Core | **Exam Frequency:** 0/7 finals · CT-03 Q01(d) & Q04 (6 marks)
> **Sources:** Silberschatz 7e Ch. 7 (§7.3.1, §7.3.3, §7.5.1) · Lecture Deck 06 (Slides 41–46) · Raidah Class Notes (pp. 22–23)

---

## Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF)

Boyce-Codd Normal Form eliminates all data redundancy caused by functional dependencies.

**Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF):** A relation schema $R$ is in BCNF with respect to functional dependency set $F$ if, for every non-trivial functional dependency $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$ in $F^+$:

$$
\alpha \text{ is a superkey of } R
$$

**Third Normal Form (3NF):** A relation schema $R$ is in 3NF if, for every non-trivial functional dependency $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$ in $F^+$, at least one of the following holds:
1. $\alpha$ is a superkey of $R$, **OR**
2. Each attribute $A \in (\beta - \alpha)$ is a **prime attribute** (member of some candidate key).

**Determinant:** The left-hand attribute set $\alpha$ in dependency $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$. Under BCNF, every determinant must be a superkey.

### Why 3NF Leaves Redundancy (The Overlapping Key Loophole)

Suppose schema $R(\text{Student}, \text{Course}, \text{Instructor})$ has overlapping candidate keys $\{\text{Student}, \text{Course}\}$ and $\{\text{Student}, \text{Instructor}\}$. All three attributes are prime.

If each instructor teaches only one course ($\text{Instructor} \rightarrow \text{Course}$):
- $\text{Instructor}$ is not a superkey.
- But $\text{Course}$ is a prime attribute.
- The relation satisfies 3NF under condition 2!
- Yet every time an instructor teaches a new student, the course name is repeated redundantly.

BCNF removes the prime-attribute escape clause entirely: $\text{Instructor}$ must be a superkey. Because it is not, the relation violates BCNF.

---

## 3NF vs. BCNF Comparison

| Feature | Third Normal Form (3NF) | Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Strictness** | Less strict (allows prime-attribute loophole) | Strictly stronger (zero FD redundancy) |
| **Condition on $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$** | $\alpha$ is superkey **OR** $\beta - \alpha$ is prime | $\alpha$ MUST be a superkey |
| **Overlapping Key Anomalies** | Permitted | Eliminated |
| **Lossless-Join Decomposition** | Always achievable | Always achievable |
| **Dependency Preservation** | Always guaranteed | Not always guaranteed |

### The Subset Relationship: BCNF $\subset$ 3NF

If relation $R$ is in BCNF, every non-trivial dependency $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$ satisfies condition 1 ($\alpha$ is a superkey). Since condition 1 of 3NF is identical to the only condition of BCNF, every BCNF relation is automatically in 3NF:

$$
\text{BCNF} \subset \text{3NF} \subset \text{2NF} \subset \text{1NF}
$$

However, a 3NF relation is not necessarily in BCNF when a non-trivial dependency relies on the prime-attribute escape clause (where $\alpha$ is not a superkey but $\beta$ is prime).

---

## Testing for the Highest Normal Form

1. **Check 1NF:** Are all attribute values atomic? If no $\rightarrow$ UNF. If yes $\rightarrow$ at least 1NF.
2. **Check 2NF:** Does any non-prime attribute depend on a proper subset of a composite candidate key? If yes $\rightarrow$ Highest is **1NF**. If no $\rightarrow$ at least 2NF.
3. **Check 3NF:** Does any non-trivial FD have a non-superkey determinant AND a non-prime dependent? If yes $\rightarrow$ Highest is **2NF**. If no $\rightarrow$ at least 3NF.
4. **Check BCNF:** Is every determinant of a non-trivial FD a superkey? If yes $\rightarrow$ Highest is **BCNF**. If no $\rightarrow$ Highest is **3NF**.

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)

### 🎯 GQ1 · CT-03 Q.01(d) · Highest Normal Form Satisfied · 02 Marks

> *CO3 · Asked in: CT-03*

**Question.** Consider $\text{ENROLLMENT}(\text{StudentID}, \text{StudentName}, \text{CourseID}, \text{CourseName}, \text{InstructorID}, \text{InstructorName}, \text{Grade})$ with functional dependencies: $\text{StudentID} \rightarrow \text{StudentName}$; $\text{CourseID} \rightarrow \text{CourseName}, \text{InstructorID}$; $\text{InstructorID} \rightarrow \text{InstructorName}$; $(\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}) \rightarrow \text{Grade}$.  
**(d)** Determine the highest normal form satisfied by the given relation. Give a proper justification.

**Answer.**

1. **First Normal Form (1NF) Check:**  
   All attributes hold atomic, single values. Therefore, the relation satisfies **1NF**.

2. **Second Normal Form (2NF) Check:**  
   The candidate key is composite: $\{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}\}$.  
   Prime attributes: $\{\text{StudentID}, \text{CourseID}\}$.  
   Non-prime attributes: $\{\text{StudentName}, \text{CourseName}, \text{InstructorID}, \text{InstructorName}, \text{Grade}\}$.

   Examining the functional dependencies:
   - $\text{StudentID} \rightarrow \text{StudentName}$: The non-prime attribute $\text{StudentName}$ depends only on $\text{StudentID}$, which is a proper subset of the candidate key.
   - $\text{CourseID} \rightarrow \text{CourseName}, \text{InstructorID}$: The non-prime attributes depend only on $\text{CourseID}$, which is a proper subset of the candidate key.

   Both dependencies are **partial functional dependencies**. Because non-prime attributes depend on proper subsets of the candidate key, the relation violates 2NF.

$$
\boxed{\text{Highest Normal Form Satisfied: First Normal Form (1NF)}}
$$

### 🎯 GQ2 · CT-03 Q.04 · Justifying BCNF vs. 3NF · 04 Marks

> *CO3 · Asked in: CT-03*

**Question.** Justify the statement: "Every BCNF relation is in 3NF, but every 3NF relation is not necessarily in BCNF."

**Answer.**

#### 1. Definitions of 3NF and BCNF

- **Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF):**  
  A relation schema $R$ is in BCNF if for every non-trivial functional dependency $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$, $\alpha$ is a **superkey** of $R$.
- **Third Normal Form (3NF):**  
  A relation schema $R$ is in 3NF if for every non-trivial functional dependency $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$, at least one of the following conditions holds:
  1. $\alpha$ is a superkey of $R$, **OR**
  2. Each attribute $A \in (\beta - \alpha)$ is a **prime attribute** (member of some candidate key).

#### 2. Why Every BCNF Relation is in 3NF

If a relation is in BCNF, every non-trivial FD $\alpha \rightarrow \beta$ satisfies condition 1 ($\alpha$ is a superkey). Since condition 1 of 3NF is identical to the only condition of BCNF, every dependency that satisfies BCNF automatically satisfies 3NF. Thus, BCNF is a stricter subset of 3NF:
$$
\text{BCNF} \subset \text{3NF}
$$

#### 3. Why Every 3NF Relation is Not Necessarily in BCNF (Counterexample)

Consider relation $R(A, B, C, D)$ with functional dependencies:
$$
F = \{AB \rightarrow CD, \; D \rightarrow A\}
$$

1. **Find Candidate Keys:**
   - $AB^+ = \{A, B, C, D\} \implies AB$ is a candidate key.
   - $DB^+ = \{D, B, A, C\} \implies BD$ is a candidate key.
   - Prime attributes: $\{A, B, D\}$.
   - Non-prime attribute: $\{C\}$.

2. **Check 3NF:**
   - $AB \rightarrow CD$: $AB$ is a superkey (satisfies condition 1).
   - $D \rightarrow A$: $D$ is not a superkey. But attribute $A$ is a **prime attribute** because it is part of candidate key $AB$ (satisfies condition 2).
   - Therefore, the relation $R$ is in **3NF**.

3. **Check BCNF:**
   - In dependency $D \rightarrow A$, the determinant $D$ is not a superkey. BCNF does not allow the prime attribute relaxation (condition 2).
   - Therefore, the relation $R$ is **NOT in BCNF**.

Because relation $R$ is in 3NF but not in BCNF, the statement is justified.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

- **The Trade-Off Rule:** If an exam asks why a designer chooses 3NF over BCNF, answer: **3NF always guarantees dependency preservation**, whereas decomposing into BCNF may lose functional dependencies.
- **Counterexample construction:** Memorize the classic 4-attribute counterexample $R(A, B, C, D)$ with $F = \{AB \rightarrow CD, D \rightarrow A\}$. Candidate keys are $AB$ and $BD$. $D \rightarrow A$ satisfies 3NF because $A$ is prime, but fails BCNF because $D$ is not a superkey.
