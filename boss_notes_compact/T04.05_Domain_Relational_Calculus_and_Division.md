# T04.5: Domain Relational Calculus and Relational Division

> **Topic:** T04 · Relational Algebra & Calculus | **Priority:** 🟢 | **Exam Frequency:** 1/7 finals (3 marks in 2020 Q.4(b))
> **Sources:** Silberschatz 7e Ch. 2 (§2.6 p. 61, Online Ch. 27) · Lecture Deck 02 (Slide 9) · Raidah Class Notes (pp. 13–14)

---

## The Three Pure Relational Languages

**Tuple Relational Calculus (TRC):** A declarative query language where variables represent entire tuples ($t$). Written as $\{\, t \mid P(t) \,\}$.

**Domain Relational Calculus (DRC):** A declarative query language where variables represent individual column values rather than whole tuples. Written as:

$$
\{\, \langle x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n \rangle \mid P(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) \,\}
$$

By Codd's Theorem, Relational Algebra, TRC, and DRC share identical expressive power when restricted to safe queries.

### Comparison of Query Formalisms

| Feature | Relational Algebra | Tuple Relational Calculus (TRC) | Domain Relational Calculus (DRC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Approach** | Procedural (step-by-step) | Declarative (what, not how) | Declarative (what, not how) |
| **Variables** | Whole relations | Row tuple variables $t$ | Column domain variables $\langle a, b, c \rangle$ |
| **Syntax** | $\Pi_L(\sigma_P(r \bowtie s))$ | $\{\, t \mid P(t) \,\}$ | $\{\, \langle x_1, \dots, x_n \rangle \mid P(x_1, \dots, x_n) \,\}$ |
| **Column Access** | Operator subscripts | Attribute notation: $t[A]$ | Positional tuple variables: $\langle a, b, c \rangle$ |

## Domain Relational Calculus (DRC) Structure

In DRC, expressions follow set-builder notation:
- **Left of the bar ($\langle x_1, \dots, x_n \rangle$):** Output domain variables. The arity must match the target schema.
- **Right of the bar ($P$):** A logical formula combining table membership ($\langle a, b, c \rangle \in r$), comparisons ($a = 10$), connectives ($\land, \lor, \neg$), and quantifiers ($\exists, \forall$).

### Translating Basic Relational Operators to DRC

Let $R = (A, B, C)$ and let $r_1, r_2$ be relations on $R$ with domain variables $\langle a, b, c \rangle$:

1. **Selection ($\sigma_{A = 10}(r_1)$):**

$$
\{\, \langle a, b, c \rangle \mid \langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_1 \land a = 10 \,\}
$$

2. **Projection ($\Pi_A(r_1)$):** Variables not appearing on the left must be existentially quantified:

$$
\{\, \langle a \rangle \mid \exists b, c \; (\langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_1) \,\}
$$

3. **Union ($r_1 \cup r_2$):**

$$
\{\, \langle a, b, c \rangle \mid \langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_1 \lor \langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_2 \,\}
$$

4. **Intersection ($r_1 \cap r_2$):**

$$
\{\, \langle a, b, c \rangle \mid \langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_1 \land \langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_2 \,\}
$$

5. **Set Difference ($r_1 - r_2$):**

$$
\{\, \langle a, b, c \rangle \mid \langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_1 \land \neg(\langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_2) \,\}
$$

6. **Cartesian Product ($r_1(A, B) \times r_2(C, D)$):**

$$
\{\, \langle a, b, c, d \rangle \mid \langle a, b \rangle \in r_1 \land \langle c, d \rangle \in r_2 \,\}
$$

### Safety of Calculus Expressions

**Safety:** A calculus query is safe if it guarantees a finite result set. Expressions like $\{\, \langle a \rangle \mid \neg(\langle a \rangle \in r) \,\}$ are unsafe because infinitely many constants outside relation $r$ satisfy the condition. Every variable in the result must come from the active domain of formula $P$.

## Relational Division ($\div$)

**Relational division ($\div$):** A derived binary operator that handles universal quantification ("for all") queries.

Let $r(R)$ and $s(S)$ be relations where $S \subset R$. The division $r \div s$ has schema $R - S$. A tuple $t$ belongs to $r \div s$ if and only if every tuple $u \in s$ combines with $t$ to form a tuple $\langle t, u \rangle \in r$:

$$
r \div s = \{\, t \mid t \in \Pi_{R-S}(r) \land \forall u \in s \; (t \cup u \in r) \,\}
$$

### Expansion via Fundamental Operators

Division is derived using projection, Cartesian product, and set difference:

$$
r \div s = \Pi_{R - S}(r) - \Pi_{R - S}\left(\left(\Pi_{R - S}(r) \times s\right) - r\right)
$$

1. **Step 1 (All Candidates):** Identify all candidate entities from $r$:

$$
T_1 \leftarrow \Pi_{R - S}(r)
$$

2. **Step 2 (Ideal Combinations):** Form all pairings of candidates with divisor requirements:

$$
T_2 \leftarrow T_1 \times s
$$

3. **Step 3 (Missing Requirements):** Subtract actual records $r$ to expose missing combinations:

$$
T_3 \leftarrow T_2 - r
$$

4. **Step 4 (Disqualified Entities):** Project candidates that missed at least one requirement:

$$
T_4 \leftarrow \Pi_{R - S}(T_3)
$$

5. **Step 5 (Surviving Winners):** Subtract disqualified candidates from all candidates:

$$
r \div s \leftarrow T_1 - T_4
$$

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)

### 🎯 GQ1 · 2020 Q.4(b) · Domain Relational Calculus Equivalents · [03 Marks]

> *Section A · Asked in: 2020 · L1 (asked once)*

**Question.** Let that, $R=(A, B, C)$ and $r_1$, $r_2$ both be relations on the schema R. Give an expression in the domain relational calculus that is equivalent to each of the followings.  
(i) $\pi_A(r_1)$  
(ii) $r_1 \cup r_2$  
(iii) $r_1 \cap r_2$

**Answer.**

Let relation instances $r_1$ and $r_2$ consist of tuples with three domain variables $\langle a, b, c \rangle$ corresponding to attributes $(A, B, C)$.

#### (i) Domain calculus for $\pi_A(r_1)$

Project attribute $A$ by existentially quantifying domain variables $b$ and $c$:

$$
\{\, \langle a \rangle \mid \exists b, c \; (\langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_1) \,\}
$$

#### (ii) Domain calculus for $r_1 \cup r_2$

A tuple belongs to the union if it belongs to $r_1$, or to $r_2$, or to both:

$$
\{\, \langle a, b, c \rangle \mid \langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_1 \lor \langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_2 \,\}
$$

#### (iii) Domain calculus for $r_1 \cap r_2$

A tuple belongs to the intersection if it belongs to both $r_1$ and $r_2$:

$$
\{\, \langle a, b, c \rangle \mid \langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_1 \land \langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_2 \,\}
$$

### 🎯 GQ2 · Likely Exam Question · Universal Query with Relational Division · [04 Marks]

> *Syllabus Unit 3 & Silberschatz 7e Ch. 2 Ex 2.9 Drill*

**Question.** Consider relations $\text{takes}(\text{ID}, \text{course\_id})$ and $\text{cs\_courses}(\text{course\_id})$.  
(a) Write a relational algebra expression using the division operator ($\div$) to find the IDs of all students who have taken all Computer Science courses.  
(b) Rewrite the query using only fundamental relational algebra operators ($\Pi, \times, -$).

**Answer.**

#### (a) Expression using the division operator ($\div$)

$$
\Pi_{\text{ID}, \text{course\_id}}(\text{takes}) \div \text{cs\_courses}
$$

#### (b) Expression using fundamental operators ($\Pi, \times, -$)

Here $R = (\text{ID}, \text{course\_id})$, $S = (\text{course\_id})$, and $R - S = (\text{ID})$:

$$
\Pi_{\text{ID}}(\text{takes}) - \Pi_{\text{ID}}\left(\left(\Pi_{\text{ID}}(\text{takes}) \times \text{cs\_courses}\right) - \Pi_{\text{ID}, \text{course\_id}}(\text{takes})\right)
$$

*Step-by-step breakdown:*
1. $\Pi_{\text{ID}}(\text{takes})$ retrieves all candidate students who have completed at least one course.
2. $\Pi_{\text{ID}}(\text{takes}) \times \text{cs\_courses}$ builds all ideal combinations of students and required courses.
3. Subtracting actual enrollments leaves combinations that candidates failed to complete.
4. Projecting $\Pi_{\text{ID}}$ isolates candidates with missing courses.
5. Subtracting failing candidates from all candidates yields only students who completed every CS course.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

- **Match Left-Side Arity:** In DRC, domain variables on the left of the bar ($\langle x_1, \dots, x_k \rangle$) must match the target arity. For $\pi_A(r_1)$, write $\langle a \rangle$, never $\langle a, b, c \rangle$.
- **Quantify Projected-Out Columns:** Forgetting existential quantifiers (writing $\{\, \langle a \rangle \mid \langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_1 \,\}$) leaves $b$ and $c$ unbound. Always write $\exists b, c \; (\langle a, b, c \rangle \in r_1)$.
- **Do Not Confuse TRC and DRC:** TRC uses row variables ($t \in r$), while DRC uses column tuple variables ($\langle a, b, c \rangle \in r$).
- **Remember the Double Subtraction in Division:** In $r \div s = T_1 - \Pi(T_2 - r)$, outer subtraction removes disqualified candidates, while inner subtraction identifies missing requirements.
