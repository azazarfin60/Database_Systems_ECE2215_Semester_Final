# T04.1: Basic Relational Algebra Operators

> **Topic:** T04 · Relational Algebra & Calculus | **Priority:** ⭐ | **Exam Frequency:** 0/7 finals (Foundation for 48 marks in T04.2–T04.3)
> **Sources:** Silberschatz 7e Ch. 2 (§2.6, pp. 48–56) · Lecture Deck 02 (Slides 9–26) · Raidah Class Notes (T04, pp. 13R–14L)

## Core Relational Algebra Concepts

**Relational algebra:** A procedural query language that forms the theoretical foundation of SQL. Operators take one or two relations as input and produce a new relation as output. Because results are relations, operations combine into nested expressions.

### Fundamental vs. Derived Operators

Relational algebra operators divide into two distinct groups:

1. **Six Fundamental Operators:**
   - Select ($\sigma$): Horizontal row filtering.
   - Project ($\Pi$): Vertical column slicing.
   - Cartesian Product ($\times$): Cross-pairing tuples across two relations.
   - Union ($\cup$): Merging tuples from two compatible relations.
   - Set Difference ($-$): Removing tuples of one relation from another.
   - Rename ($\rho$): Assigning aliases to relations or attributes.
   None of these six can be derived from each other without losing expressive power.

2. **Derived (Additional) Operators:**
   - Set Intersection ($\cap$): $r \cap s = r - (r - s)$.
   - Theta Join ($\bowtie_\theta$): $r \bowtie_\theta s = \sigma_\theta(r \times s)$.
   - Natural Join ($\bowtie$): Matches tuples on shared attribute names and eliminates duplicate columns.
   - Assignment ($\leftarrow$): Stores intermediate relations in named temporary variables.

## The Six Fundamental Operators

### 1. Select Operation ($\sigma$)

**Select operation ($\sigma$):** A unary operator that filters tuples horizontally based on a condition. Written as:

$$
\sigma_{p}(r)
$$

Here $p$ is a selection predicate and $r$ is the input relation. The result contains only tuples from $r$ that satisfy $p$.

- Predicate $p$ connects terms using logical operators: $\land$ (AND), $\lor$ (OR), $\neg$ (NOT).
- Terms compare attributes or constants using: $=, \neq, <, \le, >, \ge$.
- Example: Find Physics instructors with salary above \$90,000:

$$
\sigma_{\text{dept\_name} = \text{"Physics"} \land \text{salary} > 90000}(\text{instructor})
$$

### 2. Project Operation ($\Pi$)

**Project operation ($\Pi$):** A unary operator that selects specified attributes vertically and removes unlisted columns. Written as:

$$
\Pi_{A_1, A_2, \dots, A_k}(r)
$$

Because relations are mathematical sets, projection automatically eliminates duplicate tuples from the output.

- Example: List all unique department names from instructors:

$$
\Pi_{\text{dept\_name}}(\text{instructor})
$$

### 3. Cartesian Product Operation ($\times$)

**Cartesian product ($\times$):** A binary operator that pairs every tuple in relation $r$ with every tuple in relation $s$. Written as:

$$
r \times s
$$

- **Arity:** If $r$ has $n_1$ attributes and $s$ has $n_2$ attributes, $r \times s$ has $n_1 + n_2$ attributes.
- **Cardinality:** If $r$ has $m_1$ tuples and $s$ has $m_2$ tuples, $r \times s$ has $m_1 \times m_2$ tuples.
- Shared column names are prefixed with the table name ($r.A, s.A$) to prevent naming conflicts.

### 4. Union Operation ($\cup$)

**Union ($\cup$):** A binary operator that combines tuples from two relations into a single set, dropping duplicates. Written as:

$$
r \cup s
$$

**Union compatibility:** Two relations $r$ and $s$ are union-compatible if:
1. Both relations have the same arity (identical number of attributes).
2. The domain of attribute $i$ in $r$ matches the domain of attribute $i$ in $s$ for all $i$.

Example: Find course IDs offered in Fall 2017, Spring 2018, or both:

$$
\Pi_{\text{course\_id}}(\sigma_{\text{semester} = \text{"Fall"} \land \text{year} = 2017}(\text{section})) \cup \Pi_{\text{course\_id}}(\sigma_{\text{semester} = \text{"Spring"} \land \text{year} = 2018}(\text{section}))
$$

### 5. Set Difference Operation ($-$)

**Set difference ($-$):** A binary operator that returns tuples present in relation $r$ but absent from relation $s$. Written as:

$$
r - s
$$

Both $r$ and $s$ must be union-compatible. Use this operator for queries asking for "only", "never", or "but not".

Example: Find courses taught in Fall 2017 but not in Spring 2018:

$$
\Pi_{\text{course\_id}}(\sigma_{\text{semester} = \text{"Fall"} \land \text{year} = 2017}(\text{section})) - \Pi_{\text{course\_id}}(\sigma_{\text{semester} = \text{"Spring"} \land \text{year} = 2018}(\text{section}))
$$

### 6. Rename Operation ($\rho$)

**Rename operation ($\rho$):** A unary operator that gives an alias to a relation or its attributes. It allows self-joins and removes ambiguity.

- Rename relation only: $\rho_{x}(E)$ gives table name $x$ to expression $E$.
- Rename relation and attributes: $\rho_{x(A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n)}(E)$ renames the table to $x$ and its columns to $A_1, \dots, A_n$.

Example: Find instructors who earn more than instructor "12121":

$$
\Pi_{\text{instructor.name}}(\sigma_{\text{instructor.salary} > d.\text{salary}}(\text{instructor} \times \rho_{d}(\sigma_{\text{ID} = \text{"12121"}}(\text{instructor}))))
$$

## Derived Operators and Relation Updates

### Derived Operators

1. **Set Intersection ($\cap$):** Keeps tuples present in both relations:

$$
r \cap s = r - (r - s)
$$

2. **Theta Join ($\bowtie_\theta$):** Pairs tuples satisfying condition $\theta$:

$$
r \bowtie_\theta s = \sigma_\theta(r \times s)
$$

3. **Natural Join ($\bowtie$):** Pairs tuples with equal values on all shared attributes, keeping only one copy of each shared column:

$$
r \bowtie s = \Pi_{R \cup S}(\sigma_{\bigwedge (r.A_i = s.A_i)}(r \times s))
$$

4. **Assignment ($\leftarrow$):** Stores temporary results into named variables:

$$
\text{FallCourses} \leftarrow \Pi_{\text{course\_id}}(\sigma_{\text{semester} = \text{"Fall"} \land \text{year} = 2017}(\text{section}))
$$

### Relation Modification Operations (Silberschatz 6e Syntax)

Modifications compute target tuples and overwrite relation variables:

- **Deletion:** Subtract unwanted tuples from $r$:

$$
r \leftarrow r - E
$$

- **Insertion:** Union new tuples into $r$:

$$
r \leftarrow r \cup E
$$

- **Update:** Split relation into updated and untouched parts, then union:

$$
r \leftarrow T_{\text{updated}} \cup T_{\text{untouched}}
$$

Where $T_{\text{target}} \leftarrow \sigma_P(r)$, $T_{\text{updated}} \leftarrow \Pi_{A_1, \dots, \text{new\_val}, \dots, A_n}(T_{\text{target}})$, and $T_{\text{untouched}} \leftarrow r - T_{\text{target}}$.

## 🔮 Likely Questions

### 🎯 LQ1 · Six Fundamental Operators and Union Compatibility · [05 Marks]

> *Exam Theory Drill · High Probability for Unasked Foundations*

**Question.** List the six basic operators of relational algebra. State the conditions required for two relations to be union-compatible, and explain why this condition is necessary.

**Answer.**

#### 1. Six Basic Operators of Relational Algebra
1. **Select ($\sigma$):** Unary operator that filters rows satisfying a predicate.
2. **Project ($\Pi$):** Unary operator that outputs specified attributes and removes duplicate rows.
3. **Cartesian Product ($\times$):** Binary operator that pairs every tuple of relation $r$ with every tuple of relation $s$.
4. **Union ($\cup$):** Binary operator that merges tuples from two relations into a single set.
5. **Set Difference ($-$):** Binary operator that outputs tuples present in the first relation but absent from the second.
6. **Rename ($\rho$):** Unary operator that renames relations or attributes.

#### 2. Union Compatibility Conditions
For relations $r$ and $s$ to undergo set operations ($\cup, \cap, -$):
1. **Equal Arity:** Both relations must have the exact same number of attributes ($\text{arity}(r) = \text{arity}(s) = n$).
2. **Compatible Domains:** For every column position $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$, attribute $i$ of $r$ and attribute $i$ of $s$ must share the same data type.

#### 3. Why It Is Necessary
Relations are subsets of Cartesian products of attribute domains ($r \subseteq D_1 \times \dots \times D_n$). Without equal arity and compatible column domains, combining tuples produces ill-defined rows where values lack a common domain (for example, combining student names with monetary salaries).

### 🎯 LQ2 · Distinguish Cartesian Product, Theta Join, and Natural Join · [05 Marks]

> *Exam Theory Drill · Comparison and Equivalences*

**Question.** Differentiate among Cartesian product ($\times$), theta join ($\bowtie_\theta$), and natural join ($\bowtie$) with clear mathematical definitions and examples.

**Answer.**

| Feature | Cartesian Product ($r \times s$) | Theta Join ($r \bowtie_\theta s$) | Natural Join ($r \bowtie s$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Pairs every tuple of $r$ with every tuple of $s$. | Cartesian product followed by selection on condition $\theta$. | Matches tuples having equal values on all shared attribute names. |
| **Formula** | $r \times s$ | $\sigma_\theta(r \times s)$ | $\Pi_{R \cup S}(\sigma_{\bigwedge (r.A_i = s.A_i)}(r \times s))$ |
| **Attributes** | Retains all attributes; prefixes duplicate names ($r.A, s.A$). | Retains all attributes; prefixes duplicate names. | Drops duplicate shared attributes; keeps shared columns once. |
| **Condition** | None. | Explicit predicate $\theta$ specified by user. | Implicit equality on all identical attribute names. |
| **Tuple Count** | Always $m_1 \times m_2$. | At most $m_1 \times m_2$. | At most $m_1 \times m_2$. |

**Example:**
Let $r(A, B) = \{(1, 2)\}$ and $s(B, C) = \{(2, 3), (4, 5)\}$.
- **Cartesian Product:**

$$
r \times s = \{(1, 2, 2, 3), (1, 2, 4, 5)\} \quad \text{over schema } (r.A, r.B, s.B, s.C)
$$

- **Theta Join ($r \bowtie_{r.B < s.B} s$):**

$$
r \bowtie_{r.B < s.B} s = \{(1, 2, 4, 5)\}
$$

- **Natural Join ($r \bowtie s$):** Matches on shared attribute $B$ where $r.B = s.B = 2$:

$$
r \bowtie s = \{(1, 2, 3)\} \quad \text{over schema } (A, B, C)
$$

### 🎯 LQ3 · Relational Algebra Update and Salary Increase · [04 Marks]

> *Exam Procedure Drill · 6e Update Mechanics*

**Question.** Explain how relation modifications (insertions, deletions, and updates) are expressed in relational algebra. Show how to give a 10% salary raise to all employees in the "Hardware" department using relation assignment on $\text{works}(\text{person\_name}, \text{company\_name}, \text{salary})$.

**Answer.**

#### 1. General Relation Modification Syntax
In relational algebra, modifications compute new relation states and reassign relation variables:
- **Insertion:** $r \leftarrow r \cup E$
- **Deletion:** $r \leftarrow r - E$
- **Update:** $r \leftarrow (r - T_{\text{target}}) \cup T_{\text{updated}}$

#### 2. Salary Raise Walkthrough on `works`
1. **Identify affected employees:**

$$
T_{\text{target}} \leftarrow \sigma_{\text{company\_name} = \text{"Hardware"}}(\text{works})
$$

2. **Compute updated tuples with 10% raise:**

$$
T_{\text{raised}} \leftarrow \Pi_{\text{person\_name}, \text{company\_name}, \text{salary} * 1.1}(T_{\text{target}})
$$

3. **Isolate untouched records:**

$$
T_{\text{untouched}} \leftarrow \text{works} - T_{\text{target}}
$$

4. **Combine and reassign:**

$$
\text{works} \leftarrow T_{\text{raised}} \cup T_{\text{untouched}}
$$

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

- **Set Semantics vs. SQL Multiset:** In relational algebra, $\Pi_{A}(r)$ automatically removes duplicate rows. SQL `SELECT A FROM r` keeps duplicates unless `DISTINCT` is specified.
- **Natural Join Trap:** Natural join matches on ALL attributes with matching names. If two tables share both `ID` and `name`, $r \bowtie s$ requires both to match. If you only want to match `ID`, use theta join: $r \bowtie_{r.\text{ID} = s.\text{ID}} s$.
- **Never Forget Untouched Rows in Updates:** Writing $r \leftarrow T_{\text{updated}}$ overwrites the table with only the modified rows and deletes all other employees. Always union with $r - T_{\text{target}}$.
- **Use Assignment ($\leftarrow$) in Multi-Step Queries:** Break long expressions into named intermediate relations ($T_1, T_2$). This avoids parenthesis errors and earns full marks.
