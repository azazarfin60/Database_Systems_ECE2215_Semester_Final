## Healthcare Schema Query Patterns

**Theta join ($\bowtie_\theta$):** A binary operator defined as $\sigma_\theta(r \times s)$ that pairs tuples from $r$ and $s$ satisfying condition $\theta$.

**Natural join ($\bowtie$):** Pairs tuples having equal values on all common attribute names and removes duplicate shared columns.

**Set intersection ($\cap$):** Returns tuples that appear in both union-compatible relations ($r \cap s = r - (r - s)$).

**Set difference ($-$):** Returns tuples present in $r$ but absent from $s$ ($r - s$).

**Aggregate functions:** Mathematical summary functions ($\text{count}, \text{max}, \text{sum}, \text{avg}, \text{min}$) that take a collection of attribute values and compute a single summary value across a group or whole relation.

### Pattern 1: Managing Typographical Errors in Exam Schemas

Past RUET papers contain occasional typos (such as `Parient_ID` in 2019 and `relation database` in 2024). When writing relational algebra:
1. State a one-line assumption at the start of your answer: `*Assumption: Parient_ID in Report is a typo for Patient_ID.*`
2. Use the corrected attribute name consistently so natural joins ($\bowtie$) work properly.

### Pattern 2: Two-Stage Daily Peak Metric (Max on a Date)

To find the highest daily count without an SQL `ORDER BY ... LIMIT 1` construct, evaluate two aggregation stages using $\gamma$:

1. **Stage 1 (Count per Date):** Filter for target conditions, group by `Date`, and count patient occurrences:

$$
T_{\text{daily}} \leftarrow {}_{\text{Date}}\gamma_{\text{count}(\text{Patient\_ID}) \ \text{as} \ \text{patient\_count}}(\sigma_{\text{condition}}(\text{History}))
$$

2. **Stage 2 (Overall Maximum):** Run scalar aggregation $\gamma_{\text{max}}$ on $T_{\text{daily}}$ without grouping attributes to find the peak count:

$$
\gamma_{\text{max}(\text{patient\_count})}(T_{\text{daily}})
$$

### Pattern 3: The "Only" Constraint Pattern via Set Difference

Filtering with $\sigma_{\text{dose\_no} = 1}(\text{vaccine})$ fails when finding individuals who took the "1st dose only". Multi-dose recipients have a row for dose 1 and another row for dose 2, so selection on `dose_no = 1` includes two-dose patients.

To enforce "only", isolate all dose-1 recipients and subtract all dose-2 recipients:

1. **All Dose 1 Recipients:**

$$
T_{\text{dose1}} \leftarrow \Pi_{\text{id}}(\sigma_{\text{dose\_no} = 1}(\text{vaccine}))
$$

2. **All Dose 2 Recipients:**

$$
T_{\text{dose2}} \leftarrow \Pi_{\text{id}}(\sigma_{\text{dose\_no} = 2}(\text{vaccine}))
$$

3. **Strictly Dose 1 Only:**

$$
T_{\text{only1}} \leftarrow T_{\text{dose1}} - T_{\text{dose2}}
$$

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Always project `id` before performing set difference. If you subtract full `vaccine` rows without projecting `id`, different dose numbers and dates prevent rows from matching, so nothing is subtracted.

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)

### 🎯 GQ1 · 2019 Q.2(b) / 2024 Q.5(a) · Covid-19 Relational Database Queries · [12 Marks Total]

> *Section A (2019, 8 marks) · Section B (2024 Q.5(a), 4 marks, CO2) · L2 Merge*

**Question (2019 Q.2(b)).** Consider the following relational database.  
`Patient (Patient_ID, Patient_name, Mobile)`  
`History (Patient_ID, Symptom, Date)`  
`Report (Parient_ID, Corona_center, Test_result)`

Now write down the relational algebra (Not SQL) to find out the following queries.  
(i) Find the names of those patients who have fever symptom.  
(ii) Consider that if any patient has high-fever symptom he/she will be suspected as corona patient. Find out such maximum number of patients in a date.  
(iii) Find out the mobile numbers of those patients who have provided samples in Rajshahi corona center and their results have become positive.  
(iv) Find out the name of those patients who have high fever symptom but test result have become negative.

*(Note: Printed as "Parient_ID" in Report schema in original question paper; refers to "Patient_ID")*

**Question (2024 Q.5(a)).** Consider the following relation database:  
Patient (Patient_ID, Patient_Name, Mobile)  
History (Patient_ID, Symptom, Date)  
Report (Patient_ID, Corona_center, Test_result)  
Now, write down the relational algebra (Not SQL) to find out the following queris:  
i) Find the names of those patients who have fever symptom  
ii) Find out the mobile numbers of those patients who have provided samples in Rangpur corona center and their results have become positive.  
*(Note: "relation database" and "queris" printed verbatim in original paper; refers to relational database and queries)*

**Answer.**

*Assumption: In relation `Report`, `Parient_ID` is a typographical misprint for `Patient_ID`.*

#### (i) Names of patients who have fever symptom

Join patients with their history records filtered for fever, then project the patient names:

$$
\Pi_{\text{Patient\_name}}(\text{Patient} \bowtie \sigma_{\text{Symptom} = \text{"fever"}}(\text{History}))
$$

#### (ii) Maximum number of suspected patients (high-fever symptom) on a date

Filter records for "high-fever", count patients per date, and extract the maximum:

$$
T_1 \leftarrow \sigma_{\text{Symptom} = \text{"high-fever"}}(\text{History})
$$

$$
T_{\text{daily}} \leftarrow {}_{\text{Date}}\gamma_{\text{count}(\text{Patient\_ID}) \ \text{as} \ \text{patient\_count}}(T_1)
$$

$$
\gamma_{\text{max}(\text{patient\_count})}(T_{\text{daily}})
$$

#### (iii) Mobile numbers of patients tested in Rajshahi corona center with positive results

Filter reports for Rajshahi center and positive results, join with Patient, and project mobile numbers:

$$
\Pi_{\text{Mobile}}(\text{Patient} \bowtie \sigma_{\text{Corona\_center} = \text{"Rajshahi"} \land \text{Test\_result} = \text{"positive"}}(\text{Report}))
$$

#### (iv) Names of patients who have high fever symptom but negative test result

Find patients who have high fever and whose test result is negative:

$$
T_{\text{high}} \leftarrow \Pi_{\text{Patient\_ID}}(\sigma_{\text{Symptom} = \text{"high-fever"}}(\text{History}))
$$

$$
T_{\text{neg}} \leftarrow \Pi_{\text{Patient\_ID}}(\sigma_{\text{Test\_result} = \text{"negative"}}(\text{Report}))
$$

$$
\Pi_{\text{Patient\_name}}(\text{Patient} \bowtie (T_{\text{high}} \cap T_{\text{neg}}))
$$

*Variant for 2024 Q.5(a) (Sec B, 4 marks, CO2):*  
Answer parts (i) and (ii) only:
- **(i) Names of patients who have fever symptom:**

$$
\Pi_{\text{Patient\_name}}(\text{Patient} \bowtie \sigma_{\text{Symptom} = \text{"fever"}}(\text{History}))
$$

- **(ii) Mobile numbers of patients tested in Rangpur corona center with positive results:**

$$
\Pi_{\text{Mobile}}(\text{Patient} \bowtie \sigma_{\text{Corona\_center} = \text{"Rangpur"} \land \text{Test\_result} = \text{"positive"}}(\text{Report}))
$$

### 🎯 GQ2 · 2020 Q.2(a) · Vaccine Tracking Database & Dose-1 Only Filter · [06 Marks]

> *Section A · Asked in: 2020 · L1 (asked once)*

**Question.** Consider the following relation schema:  
  person(<u>id</u>, name, age)  
  vaccine(<u>id</u>, center, dose_no, date)  
Now give expression in relational algebra to express-  
(i) Find the names of all persons who are adult.  
(ii) Find the names of all persons who have taken the $1^{\text{st}}$ dose only.  
(iii) Find the details of all persons who were given vaccine from "RMC" center and completed $2^{\text{nd}}$ dose.

**Answer.**

*Assumption: An adult is defined as a person with $\text{age} \ge 18$. The attribute `vaccine.id` references `person.id`.*

#### (i) Names of all adult persons

$$
\Pi_{\text{name}}(\sigma_{\text{age} \ge 18}(\text{person}))
$$

#### (ii) Names of all persons who have taken the $1^{\text{st}}$ dose only

Subtract persons who took dose 2 from persons who took dose 1, then join with person to get names:

$$
T_{\text{dose1}} \leftarrow \Pi_{\text{id}}(\sigma_{\text{dose\_no} = 1}(\text{vaccine}))
$$

$$
T_{\text{dose2}} \leftarrow \Pi_{\text{id}}(\sigma_{\text{dose\_no} = 2}(\text{vaccine}))
$$

$$
T_{\text{only1}} \leftarrow T_{\text{dose1}} - T_{\text{dose2}}
$$

$$
\Pi_{\text{name}}(\text{person} \bowtie T_{\text{only1}})
$$

Equivalently, in a single expression:

$$
\Pi_{\text{name}}(\text{person} \bowtie (\Pi_{\text{id}}(\sigma_{\text{dose\_no} = 1}(\text{vaccine})) - \Pi_{\text{id}}(\sigma_{\text{dose\_no} = 2}(\text{vaccine}))))
$$

#### (iii) Details of all persons vaccinated from "RMC" center who completed $2^{\text{nd}}$ dose

Select vaccine records matching center "RMC" and dose 2, then join with person:

$$
T_{\text{rmc2}} \leftarrow \sigma_{\text{center} = \text{"RMC"} \land \text{dose\_no} = 2}(\text{vaccine})
$$

$$
\text{person} \bowtie T_{\text{rmc2}}
$$

To display person attributes only:

$$
\Pi_{\text{person.id}, \text{name}, \text{age}}(\text{person} \bowtie T_{\text{rmc2}})
$$

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

- **Never Use Simple Selection for "Only":** Do not write $\sigma_{\text{dose\_no} = 1}(\text{vaccine})$ for "dose 1 only". People with both doses satisfy that condition. Always subtract the unwanted set using set difference ($-$).
- **Project Common Keys Before Set Operations:** You cannot perform set difference between `person` and `vaccine` directly because their schemas differ. Project both to `id` first.
- **State Numeric Assumptions Explicitly:** When exams ask for "adult", write `*Assumption: adult means age >= 18.*` before your expression.
- **Use Set Intersection for Combined Criteria:** When a patient must satisfy two distinct condition tables (such as high fever in `History` and negative in `Report`), project patient IDs from each and intersect them ($T_{\text{high}} \cap T_{\text{neg}}$).
- **Remain Calm on Paper Typos:** When questions print misspellings like `Parient_ID`, note the typo and continue with standard natural joins.
