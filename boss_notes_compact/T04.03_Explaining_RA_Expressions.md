# T04.3: Explaining Relational Algebra Expressions in Words

> **Topic:** T04 · Relational Algebra & Calculus | **Priority:** 🟢 | **Exam Frequency:** 1/7 finals (+ CT-01) (4 marks final + 4 marks CT)
> **Sources:** Silberschatz 7e Ch. 2 (§2.6) · Lecture Deck 02 (Slides 15–24) · Raidah Class Notes (pp. 13–14)

## The Three-Step Translation Method

**Relational algebra:** A procedural query language where expressions evaluate from innermost parentheses outward to outermost operators.

When explaining a relational algebra formula in words on an exam script, follow this three-step blueprint:

1. **Step 1: Explain Innermost Filters:**
   **Select operation ($\sigma$):** Filters rows horizontally based on a boolean predicate. State the base relation and the exact condition used to filter tuples.
2. **Step 2: Explain Relation Combinations:**
   **Natural join ($\bowtie$):** Matches tuples having identical values on all shared attribute names.
   **Theta join ($\bowtie_\theta$):** Matches tuples satisfying explicit condition $\theta$.
   **Set difference ($-$):** Subtracts tuples of the second relation from the first.
   State which tables combine and name the exact linking attribute.
3. **Step 3: State Output Columns and Meaning:**
   **Project operation ($\Pi$):** Slices columns vertically and removes duplicate rows. State the final columns retained and provide a one-sentence summary of what the query achieves.

### Operator Translation Vocabulary

| Operator | Syntax | Mechanical Operation | English Translation Phrasing |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Selection** | $\sigma_P(r)$ | Filters tuples satisfying predicate $P$. | "Selects tuples from $r$ where condition $P$ holds." |
| **Projection** | $\Pi_L(r)$ | Retains attributes in list $L$ and removes duplicates. | "Lists unique values of columns $L$ for matching records." |
| **Natural Join** | $r \bowtie s$ | Matches tuples sharing equal values on identical column names. | "Pairs each record in $r$ with its matching record in $s$." |
| **Theta Join** | $r \bowtie_\theta s$ | Pairs tuples from $r$ and $s$ satisfying condition $\theta$. | "Combines records from $r$ and $s$ where condition $\theta$ is met." |
| **Set Difference** | $r - s$ | Retains tuples in $r$ that do not appear in $s$. | "Finds items in $r$ that do not appear in $s$." |
| **Union** | $r \cup s$ | Merges tuples from two compatible relations without duplicates. | "Finds items appearing in $r$, in $s$, or in both." |
| **Intersection** | $r \cap s$ | Retains tuples appearing in both relations simultaneously. | "Finds items that appear in both $r$ and $s$." |

## 🏆 Golden Questions (Past Exam Archive)

### 🎯 GQ1 · 2021 Q.2(c) · Explaining Takes and Student Expressions · [04 Marks]

> *Section A · CLO2 · Asked in: 2021 · L1 (asked once)*

**Question.** For each expression, explain in words what the expression does.  
Where takes(<u>id</u>, <u>course</u>, <u>sec_id</u>, sem, year, grade)  
(i) $\sigma_{year \ge 2009}(\text{takes}) \bowtie \text{student}$  
(ii) $\Pi_{id, name, course\_id}(\text{student} \bowtie \text{takes})$

**Answer.**

#### (i) Expression: $\sigma_{year \ge 2009}(\text{takes}) \bowtie \text{student}$

1. **Selection:** Filters the `takes` relation to keep only enrollment records where `year` is 2009 or later.
2. **Natural Join:** Joins the filtered course enrollments with the `student` relation on their common attribute `id`.
3. **Meaning in words:** Retrieves full student and enrollment records for all students who took a course in 2009 or later.

#### (ii) Expression: $\Pi_{id, name, course\_id}(\text{student} \bowtie \text{takes})$

1. **Natural Join:** Matches each student with the courses they have taken by joining `student` and `takes` on `id`.
2. **Projection:** Keeps only student ID (`id`), name (`name`), and course ID (`course_id`). Discards other attributes and eliminates duplicates.
3. **Meaning in words:** Lists the ID, name, and course taken for every student and each course they enrolled in.

### 🎯 GQ2 · CT-01 Q.05(iii–iv) · Explaining Theta-Join and Set Difference Expressions · [04 Marks]

> *CT-01 · CO1 · Asked in: CT-01 · L1 (asked once)*

**Question.** Describe the following relational algebra expression:  
**(iii)** $\Pi_{\text{Name}} \left(\sigma_{\text{Marks} < 50} \left(\text{STUDENT} \bowtie_{\text{STUDENT.Student\_ID} = \text{COURSE.Student\_ID}} \text{COURSE}\right)\right)$  
**(iv)** $\Pi_{\text{course\_id}} \left(\sigma_{\text{semester} = \text{"Fall"} \land \text{year} = 2017} (\text{section})\right) - \Pi_{\text{course\_id}} \left(\sigma_{\text{semester} = \text{"Spring"} \land \text{year} = 2018} (\text{section})\right)$  
*(Note: Sub-parts are numbered iii, iv on the original question script; part iv is Deck 02 Slide 24 verbatim).*

**Answer.**

#### (iii) Expression: $\Pi_{\text{Name}} \left(\sigma_{\text{Marks} < 50} \left(\text{STUDENT} \bowtie_{\text{STUDENT.Student\_ID} = \text{COURSE.Student\_ID}} \text{COURSE}\right)\right)$

1. **Theta Join:** Joins `STUDENT` and `COURSE` where `STUDENT.Student_ID = COURSE.Student_ID` to pair students with their course marks.
2. **Selection:** Filters the joined tuples to retain only records where `Marks < 50`.
3. **Projection:** Extracts the `Name` attribute and removes duplicates.
4. **Meaning in words:** Retrieves the names of all students who scored strictly less than 50 marks in at least one course.

#### (iv) Expression: $\Pi_{\text{course\_id}} \left(\sigma_{\text{semester} = \text{"Fall"} \land \text{year} = 2017} (\text{section})\right) - \Pi_{\text{course\_id}} \left(\sigma_{\text{semester} = \text{"Spring"} \land \text{year} = 2018} (\text{section})\right)$

1. **First Term:** Selects sections offered in Fall 2017 and projects their `course_id`.
2. **Second Term:** Selects sections offered in Spring 2018 and projects their `course_id`.
3. **Set Difference ($-$):** Subtracts the second set of courses from the first set.
4. **Meaning in words:** Finds all courses taught in the Fall 2017 semester that were not taught in the Spring 2018 semester.

## ⚡ Exam Tips & Common Mistakes

- **Set Difference Is Not Arithmetic:** In relational algebra, $-$ represents set difference, not arithmetic subtraction. Write "records present in the first set but absent from the second set".
- **Mention Duplicate Removal on $\Pi$:** Projection always eliminates duplicates because relations are mathematical sets. Stating this explicitly confirms theoretical accuracy.
- **Name Shared Join Attributes:** When explaining natural join ($\bowtie$), state the linking attribute name (such as `id` or `Student_ID`).
- **Follow a 3-Point Structure:** Structure your answer clearly:
  1. What the inner selection or join filters.
  2. How intermediate relations combine.
  3. A one-sentence plain-English summary of the final output.
